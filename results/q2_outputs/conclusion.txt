(a) Under the fairness criterion fixed in advance (equal parameters: MLP-M 1,128,815 vs CNN 1,130,378; same data, no augmentation, same optimiser, schedule, epochs and batch size; same LR grid chosen on validation; 3 paired seeds), the CNN reaches 87.8% test accuracy against 55.1% for the MLP (+32.7 pp). The MLP fits 99.9% of its training subset (CNN 100.0%), so the gap is one of generalisation, not of fitting. Its first-layer units are whole-image templates while the CNN's are 3x3 detectors reused everywhere. Proposed reason: locality and weight sharing let the CNN exploit the correlations between neighbouring pixels with reusable local features, which the MLP has to relearn at every position.

(b) Expected beforehand: no change for the MLP, and a large CNN drop to about the MLP's level. Observed on the test set: MLP-M -0.3 pp (within the noise band of ±1.0 pp), CNN -35.5 pp; the CNN-MLP gap goes from +32.7 pp to -2.5 pp. This is the predicted pattern. With parameters, FLOPs, budget and the pixel values themselves unchanged, the CNN's advantage disappears once neighbourhoods are destroyed, so it depends on local spatial structure. This supports the reason from (a) and rules out extra compute or easier optimisation as the explanation.

(c) E-local (local features) vs E-global (global arrangement). With per-image random tile shuffling, applied in the same way to training and test images: t=16: CNN 79.1% (R=0.75), MLP 40.2%, t=8: CNN 66.7% (R=0.41), MLP 32.1%, t=4: CNN 56.6% (R=0.12), MLP 29.0%; clean CNN 87.8%, permuted CNN 52.3%. At t=8 the CNN retains R = 0.41 of its spatial benefit, and the CNN-MLP gap is +34.6 pp (clean +32.7 pp). Confidence: the CNN's correct predictions have mean confidence 0.973 (clean), 0.938 (tile8) and 0.919 (perm), so Rc = 0.36. The AUROC of confidence for separating right from wrong is 0.894 / 0.776 / 0.722, and the ECE is 0.074 / 0.230 / 0.346. The classes that lose most for the CNN at t=8 are deer, cat, bird. The MLP changes by -23.0 pp at t=8 although it was unaffected by the fixed permutation: its features depend on content appearing at consistent absolute positions. Decision (pre-registered rule R(8) >= 0.5 not met): E-GLOBAL is supported. Randomising the arrangement of 8-px tiles removes most of the CNN's advantage, so it depends on how local parts are arranged across the image. The confidence check agrees.

Conclusion linking the three experiments: (a) at equal parameters and budget the CNN generalises better although both models fit their training data, which points to an inductive-bias explanation; (b) destroying neighbourhoods while keeping every pixel value removes the CNN's advantage and leaves the MLP unchanged, which tests that explanation directly; (c) shows that local structure alone is not enough: the CNN also relies on how 8-px parts are arranged, so its advantage combines local feature reuse with sensitivity to global configuration.
